§ 01 — About

We are a boutique consulting studio. We build production AI systems — agents, copilots, retrieval, vision — and we take on a small number of engagements each quarter so we can do the work properly.

§ 02 — Who we are

A handful of senior practitioners. No juniors on the slide deck.

Our team has shipped AI in production at scale — fraud detection, autonomous vehicles, search relevance, document understanding. We left larger orgs because we wanted to do the work, not manage the work.

Every engagement is run by the people who do the building. There is no "delivery team" you get handed off to. There is no offshore second wave. There are five of us, and we like it that way.

We're allergic to AI theatre — the pilots that never ship, the demos that fall apart on real data, the dashboards nobody opens. We build the boring half that makes the impressive half work.

§ 03 — Principles

How we work.

Six principles we keep coming back to. Most are unfashionable. All of them, in our experience, are how good AI systems actually get built.

01

Smaller than you think.

The biggest mistake teams make is scoping AI projects like SaaS projects. We start with the smallest version of the system that creates real value, and ship that in weeks — not the all-singing version in quarters.

02

Production from day one.

No "pilot" environments that look nothing like reality. We build into your stack, on your data, with your auth, from the first commit. The model is the easy part — the integration is the work.

03

Evals before models.

If you cannot measure it, you cannot improve it. Every project starts with an evaluation harness. We know our system is better because the numbers say so, not because the demo went well.

04

Boring infrastructure, interesting outputs.

We pick the dullest stack that does the job. Postgres before Pinecone. SQL before LangChain. The interesting part should be what the system does — not what it's built on.

05

Humans in the loop, by design.

The best AI systems make humans faster, not redundant. We design for escalation, override and review — because the systems that earn trust are the ones that know when to ask.

06

We hand it over.

Every engagement ends with your team owning the system — code, docs, eval harness, runbook. We are not interested in being indispensable. We are interested in being effective.

§ 04 — Engagement

A typical arc.

Most engagements run six to twelve weeks, with the same shape. We resist projects that don't fit this rhythm.

WK 01

Framing.

Two intensive days on-site. We map the workflow, the users, the data, the success criteria. We agree on what "shipped" means.

Workshop
WK 02–03

Spike.

A working prototype on real data, in your environment. Rough at the edges, honest about what works and what doesn't.

Build
WK 04–06

Production build.

Eval harness, observability, guardrails, the integration with your systems. The unglamorous 70% of any real AI deployment.

Engineer
WK 07–08

Rollout & handover.

Phased launch to real users. We sit alongside your team, tune the system, document the runbook, hand over the keys.

Deliver

The studio exists to build the thing — not the deck, not the pilot, not the proof-of-concept. The thing.
