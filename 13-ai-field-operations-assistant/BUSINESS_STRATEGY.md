# Business Strategy: AI Field Operations Assistant

## The Business Problem You Are Solving

Field service companies deploy technicians, installers, inspectors, and repair crews who spend 40-50% of their time on administrative work: writing service notes, filling out work orders, sending customer updates, and preparing reports. This happens after every service visit and typically results in delayed paperwork, inconsistent documentation, and communication gaps between technicians, dispatch, and customers.

The operational cost is invisible but real: technicians who can do 4 service calls per day are doing 3 because of admin overhead. Customer communication lags by hours. Work orders come back with missing parts or labor details that AP has to chase down.

## Target Buyers and Titles

Primary decision makers:
- VP of Field Operations or Field Service Director
- COO (for service-heavy businesses)
- CEO of mid-size field service company

Secondary influencers:
- Dispatch managers
- Customer success or service coordinators
- Operations managers

## How To Pitch This

Opening line:

"Field service companies I work with tell me their technicians spend an hour per day on admin work after each job. That is capacity being consumed by documentation instead of service delivery. I build AI field assistants that turn a few lines of technician notes into a complete work order, customer update, and follow-up plan in under 2 minutes."

Key messages:
- Free technicians from administrative overhead
- Improve customer communication after every service event
- Produce cleaner work orders for billing and dispatch
- Reduce follow-up service visits with better first-visit documentation

Discovery questions:
- How long does it take your technicians to complete paperwork after a typical service call?
- What percentage of work orders come back with incomplete or missing information?
- How do customers currently get notified after a service visit?
- What does a delayed or incomplete work order cost you in billing and follow-up?

## Why They Choose You Over a Large Firm

FSM (field service management) platforms like ServiceMax, Salesforce Field Service, and ServiceNow charge $150-$300/user/month plus 6-12 month implementations. Your approach focuses on AI augmentation of the documentation and communication layer, plugging into existing dispatch and service systems.

## Engagement and Pricing Model

Phase 1: Discovery (1-2 weeks)
- Map technician workflow, note formats, and customer communication process
- Price: $7,000 - $12,000

Phase 2: Pilot build (4-6 weeks)
- Technician note intake (text)
- Work summary and parts/labor extraction
- Customer update draft generation
- Follow-up recommendation engine
- Price: $35,000 - $70,000

Phase 3: Hardening (4-8 weeks)
- Mobile-optimized interface for field use
- Integration with dispatch or service management system
- Billing export workflow
- Price: $35,000 - $80,000

Phase 4: Retainer
- Template and rule updates
- Price: $2,500 - $5,000/month

Typical total engagement: $70,000 - $155,000

## ROI Story To Tell

For a company with 20 field technicians spending 1 hour/day on admin at $75/hour:
- Current cost: 20 × 1 × $75 × 250 days = $375,000/year
- Post-implementation (70% reduction): $112,500/year
- Annual savings: $262,500
- Plus: if each technician can add half a service call per day → revenue impact far exceeds implementation cost
- Payback period on $60K pilot: 3 months

## Ideal First Client Profile

- Field service company with 10-100 technicians
- Technicians writing manual service notes today
- Dispatch and billing systems that need cleaner work order input
- Operations leader frustrated by paperwork backlog and documentation inconsistency
