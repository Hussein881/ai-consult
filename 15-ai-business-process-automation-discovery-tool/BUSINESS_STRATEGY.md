# Business Strategy: AI Business Process Automation Discovery Tool

## The Business Problem You Are Solving

Every company knows they should be using AI and automation to improve operations. Almost none of them know where to start. Without a structured discovery methodology, companies either chase the wrong use cases (flashy but low-ROI), get stuck in analysis paralysis, or start building without a business case. Budget for AI initiatives gets wasted on proof-of-concepts that never reach production.

This is also your highest-leverage consulting offer because it is the front of every other engagement. A properly conducted automation discovery often reveals 5-10 concrete opportunities, each of which becomes a separate engagement.

## Target Buyers and Titles

Primary decision makers:
- CEO / COO (for company-wide programs)
- VP Operations (for operational workflows)
- CTO (when technology decisions are in scope)

Secondary influencers:
- Business analysts
- Operations managers
- Digital transformation leads
- Department heads

## How To Pitch This

Opening line:

"Most companies investing in AI don't have a reliable method to find and prioritize the right use cases first. The result is wasted budget on projects that don't deliver ROI. I offer a structured automation discovery engagement that identifies your highest-value opportunities, scores them by ROI and feasibility, and delivers an implementation roadmap, not a research report."

Key messages:
- Get certainty before committing capital to AI projects
- Identify 5-10 specific automation opportunities with estimated ROI
- Prioritize by impact, feasibility, and implementation cost
- Walk away with an actionable roadmap, not a generic presentation

Discovery questions:
- Where are your teams spending the most time on manual, repetitive tasks?
- Have you started any automation or AI projects that stalled or failed to deliver?
- What would it mean to your business to recover 10-15% of operational labor cost?
- What is your current budget approval process for technology investments?

## Why This Is Your Most Strategic Offering

This engagement is a business-development multiplier. Every discovery you conduct reveals additional implementation projects. A $15,000 discovery engagement routinely surfaces $200K-$500K in follow-on work for a capable firm. It is also the easiest offer to sell because the client is paying you to figure out what to build, not committing to a large implementation upfront.

## Engagement and Pricing Model

Phase 1: Discovery engagement (3-4 weeks)
- Guided stakeholder interviews (4-8 sessions)
- Current-state workflow mapping
- Automation opportunity identification and scoring
- ROI modeling for top 5-10 opportunities
- Deliverable: executive-ready audit report and implementation roadmap
- Price: $12,000 - $25,000

Phase 2: Pilot implementation of top priority (4-8 weeks)
- Build the highest-ROI opportunity identified in Phase 1
- Price depends on complexity: $40,000 - $120,000

Downstream engagements:
- Each additional opportunity from the discovery becomes a separate pilot
- At 3-5 pilots per discovery: $120,000 - $600,000 total follow-on
- Long-term: ongoing retainer for support and expansion

## ROI Story To Tell

This is not an ROI conversation for the client. The pitch is certainty and de-risking:

"You are going to invest in AI and automation this year. The question is whether you invest in the right projects. This discovery engagement costs $15,000 and gives you a prioritized roadmap with ROI estimates for every opportunity before you commit to a single implementation."

For the client, the cost of a failed AI implementation is $100K-$500K. The discovery costs $12K-$25K. It is a no-brainer risk management investment.

## Competitive Positioning

McKinsey, Deloitte, and Accenture offer similar "AI opportunity assessments" at $150K-$400K. Your offer delivers the same structured methodology with senior attention at a fraction of the cost, with a faster 3-4 week timeline and a clear path to implementation.

## Ideal First Client Profile

- Company with 50-500 employees and visible manual process overhead
- Operations or finance leader who has budget for AI but not a clear plan
- Company that has failed at or stalled on a previous AI initiative
- CEO or COO who has made AI a stated priority but has not committed to specific projects
