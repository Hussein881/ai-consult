# Eric Wallen Client Intro Playbook

## Objective

Use the coffee meeting with Eric Wallen as a market-discovery and referral-activation conversation.

The primary goal is not to sell Eric a project. The goal is to make him comfortable introducing you to buyers because he sees that you:

- understand business pain, not just AI tooling
- can speak credibly with operational and technical leaders
- have a low-risk offer that is easy to explain to clients
- are focused on measurable outcomes instead of vague AI transformation language

## What Eric's Position Signals

Eric is likely more valuable as a channel partner, market interpreter, and source of warm introductions than as a direct buyer.

His role suggests that he has:

- long-term relationships with hiring managers, CTOs, engineering leaders, and operations leaders
- direct exposure to the problems companies describe when they cannot hire or organize their way out of a workflow bottleneck
- credibility with clients who already trust his judgment
- pattern recognition about which business pains create budget, urgency, and willingness to take a first meeting

This means your job is to show that you are commercially credible, easy to introduce, and focused on pains that buyers already recognize.

## How To Position Yourself

Use language like this:

> I am building a consulting firm focused on practical AI deployments for companies that want measurable business outcomes, not generic AI experimentation. My background is 5 years at IBM plus my PhD, and my focus is on workflows where teams lose time, consistency, and revenue because too much work is still manual. I start with narrow, high-ROI pilots in areas like engineering productivity, support operations, internal knowledge systems, reporting automation, and decision-support workflows.

Avoid language like this:

- I want to help companies use AI to grow
- I do AI strategy and agents for businesses
- I can build anything with AI

Those phrases are too broad and do not signal a clear buyer problem.

I help companies identify high-friction workflows where teams are losing time, consistency, and decision speed, then turn those workflows into practical AI-assisted systems that reduce manual work and improve operating performance.

- Engineering teams spending too much time triaging logs, test failures, incidents, and technical documentation

- Internal knowledge trapped across documents, tickets, chat threads, and disconnected systems
- Compliance, onboarding, and documentation workflows that are slow, inconsistent, and expensive to maintain
- Decision workflows where leaders are waiting on manual synthesis before they can act

- AI test failure analysis for CI/CD and release teams
- AI log triage and root-cause support for engineering and platform teams
- Internal knowledge assistants for company documents and technical operations
- Proposal, quote, and reporting automation for business teams
- Compliance and quality documentation assistants for regulated or process-heavy environments

## How I Work

I start with a low-risk phased model:

1. Discovery diagnostic: map the workflow, quantify pain, identify success metrics
2. Pilot build: deliver a scoped AI-assisted solution with clear acceptance criteria
3. Production hardening: integrate, secure, measure, and operationalize
4. Support and expansion: improve adoption and extend into adjacent workflows

## Why Buyers Work With Me

- Direct senior attention
- Faster execution than a large consultancy
- Clear business framing, not just technical experimentation
- Lower-risk pilot structure with measurable outcomes
- Strong technical depth from IBM experience plus research background

## Good First-Fit Buyer Profiles

- CTO or VP Engineering dealing with delivery friction and operational drag
- Head of QA, platform, or release engineering facing triage overload
- COO or operations leader managing repetitive knowledge work
- Head of support, onboarding, compliance, or reporting with manual process pain
- Technology leaders in financial services or B2B software who want focused workflow improvement instead of a large transformation program

## What Makes A Good First Engagement

The best first project is one where:

- the workflow is painful and repetitive
- the business cost of delay is visible
- the users are known
- the data sources are identifiable
- success can be measured within 4 to 8 weeks

## Coffee Meeting Script

Use the flow below to keep the meeting commercially grounded.

### 1. Opening: 60-90 second framing

Say:

> Thanks again for making time. I wanted to get your perspective because you spend a lot of time hearing directly from companies about where they are struggling. I am building a consulting firm around practical AI deployments, and I want to make sure I am solving problems businesses actually pay to fix. My focus is not generic AI hype. I want to help companies improve high-friction workflows where teams lose time, consistency, and decision speed.

Transition:

> Since you have a much clearer market view than I do, I would love to understand what patterns you are seeing.

### 2. Market discovery questions

Ask:

- What technology or operations problems do your clients repeatedly complain about but struggle to solve?
- Where do you see companies trying to hire more people for a problem that might actually be a workflow or tooling problem?
- Which roles are feeling the most pain right now: CTOs, engineering leaders, QA, operations, compliance, support?
- Where are companies talking about AI but not actually getting it into production?
- What kinds of problems feel urgent enough that a buyer might approve a pilot in the next 30 to 90 days?

Transition:

> That is helpful. Let me test a few concrete wedges against what you are seeing.

### 3. Test your initial service wedges

Say:

> The areas I think I can be strongest in are engineering productivity, internal knowledge systems, workflow automation, reporting, and decision support. For example, I could help with things like AI-assisted test failure triage, log analysis, internal document assistants, support triage, or compliance and reporting workflows.

Ask:

- Which of those sounds closest to problems your clients already understand and budget around?
- Which of those would be easiest for you to explain to a client without it sounding experimental?
- Which of those feels most relevant to your financial-services and technical contacts?

Transition:

> I want to make sure the offer is narrow enough to be easy to buy.

### 4. Refine positioning and buyer language

Ask:

- If you were introducing me, what exact problem statement would get someone to take the meeting?
- What language would make you more confident sending me to one of your clients?
- What wording would make buyers tune out or assume this is just another AI pitch?
- Which buyer titles would you target first for an offer like this?

Transition:

> That helps a lot. I would rather lead with one focused offer than try to sound broad.

### 5. Ask for introductions the right way

Say:

> If, after this conversation, two or three people come to mind who deal with operational bottlenecks, engineering workflow pain, or manual decision workflows, I would appreciate an introduction. I am not looking to push a hard sell. I would be happy to start with a short conversation to learn what they are dealing with and see whether there is a fit for a focused pilot.

Optional follow-up ask:

> If it is helpful, I can send you a short one-pager you can forward so the positioning is easy to explain.

### 6. Close the meeting cleanly

Say:

> This was exactly what I needed. My next step is to sharpen the offer around the pains you think are most real and easiest to introduce. I will send you a short summary and a forwardable blurb so it is easy if someone comes to mind.

## Sharper Consulting Offer For Technical Recruiting And Financial-Services Contacts

The offer needs to be specific enough that Eric can repeat it in one sentence.

### Recommended Core Offer

Practical AI workflow diagnostic and pilot for technical and operations teams.

Working version:

> I help technical and operations leaders identify one costly manual workflow where AI can reduce analysis time, improve consistency, or speed up decisions, then I deliver a focused pilot with measurable ROI.

### Why This Fits Eric's Network

It aligns with the kinds of pains his clients are likely to describe:

- teams overloaded with manual analysis and coordination work
- difficulty hiring enough people to keep up with workflow complexity
- pressure to improve productivity without expanding headcount at the same rate
- interest in AI, but uncertainty about where it can be deployed safely and usefully

### Best Entry Offers For His Contacts

#### 1. AI Workflow Diagnostic

Best for first conversations.

- Format: 1 to 2 weeks fixed fee
- Outcome: workflow map, pain quantification, candidate use cases, recommended pilot, ROI hypothesis
- Buyers: CTO, COO, Head of Operations, VP Engineering, Head of Support, Head of QA

#### 2. Engineering Productivity Pilot

Best for software and platform teams.

- Examples: test failure analysis, log triage, incident summarization, technical knowledge assistant
- Outcome: reduced triage effort, faster release or incident response decisions, clearer knowledge access
- Buyers: CTO, VP Engineering, Head of Platform, Head of QA, Release Engineering leader

#### 3. Operations Knowledge And Reporting Pilot

Best for financial-services and process-heavy organizations.

- Examples: reporting automation, document intelligence, policy lookup, onboarding support, internal procedure assistants
- Outcome: lower manual effort, faster turnaround, more consistent output, easier auditing
- Buyers: COO, operations leader, compliance leader, PMO, business systems leader

#### 4. Compliance And Documentation Workflow Pilot

Best for regulated environments.

- Examples: evidence assembly, quality documentation drafting, policy summarization, review support workflows
- Outcome: faster document cycles, more consistency, lower administrative drag, better traceability
- Buyers: compliance, quality, operations, risk, program management

### Tailored Angle For Financial Services Contacts

For financial-services-adjacent buyers, emphasize controlled workflow improvement, not experimental automation.

Use language like:

- reduce manual review effort in process-heavy workflows
- improve consistency and auditability in documentation and reporting tasks
- accelerate internal decision support without removing human oversight
- help teams handle higher operational volume without matching headcount growth

Avoid leading with:

- autonomous agents
- fully automated decision making
- AI transformation
- replacing teams

### Suggested Buyer-Friendly Positioning Statement

> I work with technical and operational teams that know where work is slowing down but have not yet translated that into a practical AI pilot. I help them choose one workflow where the pain is measurable, the data is available, and the outcome can be proven quickly.

### Your Best Ask From Eric

Ask for introductions to people who fit one of these profiles:

- engineering leaders with release, incident, or knowledge bottlenecks
- operations leaders with repetitive document, routing, or reporting workflows
- financial-services or B2B software managers trying to improve throughput without a large transformation program

That is a better ask than requesting introductions to anyone interested in AI.

Treat Eric as a trusted market advisor and introducer.

If you make him confident that you understand buyer pain, can describe a low-risk offer clearly, and will handle his relationships well, he can become a meaningful source of qualified conversations.
