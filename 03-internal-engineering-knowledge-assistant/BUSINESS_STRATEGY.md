# Business Strategy: Internal Engineering Knowledge Assistant

## The Business Problem You Are Solving

Engineering organizations accumulate enormous amounts of institutional knowledge in docs, wikis, Confluence pages, GitHub READMEs, Slack threads, Jira tickets, and runbooks. That knowledge is impossible to search effectively. Junior engineers spend 30-60 minutes finding answers that a senior engineer can recall in 30 seconds. Senior engineers get interrupted multiple times per day with questions that are already documented somewhere. Onboarding takes weeks longer than it should.

The hidden cost is not just time. It is decision quality. Teams make architectural choices or operational decisions without access to the history and reasoning stored in old docs. They recreate work already done.

## Target Buyers and Titles

Primary decision makers:
- VP Engineering / CTO
- Head of Developer Experience or Platform
- Engineering Director

Secondary influencers:
- Staff and principal engineers
- Technical program managers
- Onboarding and learning-and-development leads

## How To Pitch This

Opening line:

"Senior engineers at companies your size typically field 5-10 knowledge questions per day from their teams. I build internal AI assistants that answer those questions from your own docs so senior engineers can stay focused on high-value work."

Key messages that land:
- Cut onboarding time by 30-50%
- Reduce senior engineer interrupt load by 60%
- Give every engineer access to institutional knowledge, not just the people who happen to know it
- Answers are grounded in your actual docs, not hallucinated

Discovery questions:
- Where does your team store technical documentation today?
- How long does onboarding take for a new engineer to become productive?
- What are the most common repetitive questions senior engineers get asked?
- Do you have a search tool today? How well does it actually work?

## Why They Choose You Over a Large Firm

Large firms would propose a knowledge management platform overhaul at $400K-$900K. Your offer is a targeted RAG assistant deployed against existing doc sources in 4-6 weeks, with measurable onboarding and productivity metrics from day one.

## Engagement and Pricing Model

Phase 1: Discovery (1-2 weeks)
- Audit doc sources, search behavior, and knowledge gap patterns
- Define top 20-30 use-case questions
- Price: $8,000 - $12,000

Phase 2: Pilot build (4-6 weeks)
- Ingest 2-4 doc sources (Confluence, GitHub, PDFs)
- Build retrieval and answer pipeline with citations
- Deploy internal interface
- Acceptance criteria: answer quality on agreed question set
- Price: $40,000 - $80,000

Phase 3: Hardening and expansion (4-8 weeks)
- Add more doc sources
- Add access control and audit logging
- Slack or Teams integration
- Price: $40,000 - $100,000

Phase 4: Retainer
- Re-indexing as docs update
- Quality monitoring
- Price: $3,000 - $6,000/month

Typical total engagement: $80,000 - $180,000

## ROI Story To Tell

For a 50-engineer org where senior engineers spend 1.5 hours/day on interrupts at $250/hour:
- Current cost: 10 senior engineers × 1.5h × $250 × 250 days = $937,500/year
- Post-implementation: reduce interrupts by 60% → save $562,500/year
- Payback period on $70K pilot: 6 weeks
