# Business Strategy: Internal Knowledge Assistant for Company Documents

## The Business Problem You Are Solving

Every company beyond 50 employees has a version of the same problem: policies, procedures, SOPs, HR documents, compliance guidelines, and operational handbooks spread across SharePoint, Google Drive, Confluence, shared drives, and email threads. Nobody can find anything reliably. New employees get bad or inconsistent answers to basic questions. Managers spend time answering questions that are already documented. Policy interpretation varies across offices and teams.

The compliance dimension makes this especially urgent in regulated industries. If employees cannot reliably access and understand the current version of a policy, companies face audit risk, liability exposure, and inconsistent enforcement.

## Target Buyers and Titles

Primary decision makers:
- Chief Operating Officer (COO)
- VP of Human Resources or Chief People Officer
- Chief Compliance Officer
- CTO or Head of IT (for technical deployment)

Secondary influencers:
- HR business partners
- Operations managers
- Internal audit leads
- Department heads

## How To Pitch This

Opening line:

"Companies at your stage typically have documentation spread across 4-6 different systems. When employees can not find authoritative answers, they either make things up or escalate to HR and managers who answer the same questions every week. I build internal AI assistants that give employees accurate, source-grounded answers from company-approved documents."

Key messages:
- Reduce HR and manager time on repetitive policy questions
- Give employees one place to find authoritative answers
- Reduce policy inconsistency and interpretation risk
- Improve compliance and audit readiness

Discovery questions:
- Where do employees go today when they have a policy or procedure question?
- How much time do HR or compliance staff spend answering repetitive document questions?
- Do you have compliance or audit requirements that depend on employees accessing current documentation?
- What are the 10 most common questions employees ask that are already documented somewhere?

## Why They Choose You Over a Large Firm

Large firms pitch enterprise content management transformations at $500K-$1.5M. Your offer deploys a working AI knowledge assistant in 4-8 weeks against existing documents, without migrating to a new content management system.

## Engagement and Pricing Model

Phase 1: Discovery (1-2 weeks)
- Audit document sources, access control needs, and top question categories
- Price: $8,000 - $12,000

Phase 2: Pilot build (4-6 weeks)
- Ingest 3-5 document sources
- Build employee-facing Q&A interface with source citations
- Basic access control and logging
- Price: $40,000 - $80,000

Phase 3: Hardening and expansion (4-8 weeks)
- Additional document sources and departments
- RBAC for confidential documents
- Admin interface for document management
- Slack or Teams integration
- Price: $40,000 - $90,000

Phase 4: Retainer
- Re-indexing as documents update
- Price: $3,000 - $6,000/month

Typical total engagement: $80,000 - $175,000

## ROI Story To Tell

For a 200-person company where HR and managers spend 5 hours/week answering repetitive document questions at $100/hour:
- Current cost: 10 employees × 5h × $100 × 50 weeks = $250,000/year
- Post-implementation (60% reduction): $100,000/year
- Annual savings: $150,000
- Payback period on $65K pilot: 5 months

Plus: reduced compliance risk and audit-preparation time add additional value.

## Ideal First Client Profile

- Mid-size company (50-500 employees)
- Multiple document systems (SharePoint, Confluence, Google Drive)
- Active HR or compliance function
- Recent audit, compliance review, or onboarding problem
- COO or HR leader who wants to scale operations without proportional headcount
